ZǏRUÌ KǑNG 孔子睿

I am an M.A. candidate in Quantitative Methods in the Social Sciences at Columbia University, where my training centers on statistics, survey methodology, causal inference, and computational methods for social data. I completed my B.A. in Economics at The Ohio State University, with coursework in econometrics, Python, calculus, linear algebra, and statistics, and studied as a visiting student at Peking University’s Guanghua School of Management, where I focused on machine learning, time-series modeling, and quantitative investment. My background combines rigorous quantitative training with an applied interest in how social data can be used to understand and address real-world problems.

My doctoral interests center on quantitative social science, social-science data analysis, and informatics. I aim to use rigorous statistical and computational methods to study public health and health disparities, economic inequality and social stratification, and the relationship between climate change and population health.

Away from research, I enjoy management simulation games such as Crusader Kings, Football Manager, and Cities: Skylines, and my favorite sport is football — I support Arsenal.
